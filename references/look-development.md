# Look development at play size

## Brief the picture

Write one sentence with a place, a time, a tangible material and a change in feeling. Pick a contradiction that can survive a small screen: a lonely night watch with a friendly shop-window glow; a sunny lunch counter with the mounting pressure of tickets. Ask where the game lives on a calm-to-busy and intimate-to-distant spectrum. A deliberately plain or unsettling direction can be the right answer.

Pick one historically or physically grounded thing to study alongside the games: the edge of an enamel tray, a harbor signal pennant, the smudged corner of a used paper ticket. Note who uses it and where wear accumulates. Translate one property into a repeatable drawing rule, such as broad reflections on enamel and dry broken edges on paper. A universal texture overlay makes those surfaces converge.

## Paint a key before building the kit

Make a small playable crop and a wide establishing frame with the same actor. Keep an approved shape constant while comparing treatments. For radically different alternatives, identify which variables changed together.

| Lens | Put it on the page | How to judge it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Composition | Make a two- or three-value thumbnail. Draw the path from the first focal shape to the secondary landmark and leave somewhere for the eye to rest. | At a glance, can you locate the actor and describe the place? If every corner has a frame and an accent, remove one competing edge. |
| Depth and negative space | Overlap a near silhouette, an actionable middle plane and a quieter distant mass. Keep a gap around the most expressive part of the pose. | Move the actor to the edge, behind the tallest prop and onto the busiest patch. Judge where losing a contour creates mystery and where it loses the target. |
| Light and palette | Key the frame from one believable source. Assign a dominant family, a counter-temperature and one accent with a job. Decide what happens to the shadow color across materials. | Turn the picture to grayscale, then restore color. A warm highlight on every object erases a lamp's power to attract the eye. Try borrowing the accent back from the scenery. |
| Shape and line | Give structural objects and living actors different rhythms of straights, curves and gaps. Decide which interior marks vanish at the smallest size. | Compare two actors as solid silhouettes, then bring back just one identifying feature each. Check the actual portrait, world sprite and HUD sizes. |
| Surface and edge | Map matte, wet, soft and hard surfaces. Place one sharp edge at the contact that matters. Let soft or broken edges recede into neighboring color. | Crop the frame to a small object. Can the viewer tell a brass catch from a paper tab by the light shape? Fix the highlight and contact shadow before adding grain. |
| Lettering and symbols | Pick numerals whose counters remain open on the game's surface. Match icon weight, shadow direction and active shape to the world material. | Try a narrow glyph beside a full disk, a two-digit count and the longest locale. The world can feel handmade while live text retains clean edges and spacing. |

## Compose atmosphere over a scene

Place three keys side by side: arrival, disturbance and release. Lock the viewpoint or an anchor so the comparison tells you something. In the disturbance key, choose *where* the frame tightens, which distant contour is swallowed, and how much color the player still gets. In release, let only some details return. A whole-frame hue change cannot by itself make a storm feel close.

**Post office by the water, an original exercise.** In calm weather, use open river space to separate the clerk from the ferry. As weather closes in, let the far bank merge and leave one warm light beside the clerk. In the generated middle key, rain makes a broad diagonal field while the warm lamp and its narrow reflection hold the near pier. In the right key, the rain marks disappear and a bright rim runs across the dock. The figure and ferry stay recognizable even as the far bank softens. As a next experiment, reduce the bright ferry windows if they pull attention from the waiting clerk. Try one wave cycle and one irregular sound against each key. Compare the impression left by several seconds of silence. [Harbor color script](../assets/studies/harbor-color-script.webp)

Ask two distinct questions in review: “Where did you look?” and “What did this place feel like?” A player can find the button instantly and still feel no curiosity or tension. Preserve intent in the next edit by naming a physical property: widen the still area around the lone figure, lower the edge contrast in the far roofline, keep the lamplight localized. Record differing reactions as reactions.

**A quieter alternative in a midnight laundromat.** Keep the approved clerk, three dryer doors and empty entrance. Compare a fluorescent pool over the clerk with a narrow amber reflection from the street. The first suggests a late shift winding down. In the second, a shoe-width interruption in the sill light can suggest someone just outside. In a 320-pixel thumbnail with stand-in shapes, a broad amber wedge competed with the clerk's face. Shorten the wedge and keep the interruption on the sill. The intent is anticipation. Ask whether it feels like company arriving or danger approaching; soften the shadow if the latter dominates. This is a hypothetical study, not a playtest in a shipped laundromat game.

## Build a small family with an author's hand

Choose a motif rooted in the world, then vary its expression. The diagonal cancellation mark of a post office might appear once on a crate, once as a pressed-button mark and once in the outcome stamp. Change scale, material and emphasis; give ordinary surfaces permission to be plain. Draw one worn corner or a brushed repair where the world suggests a history. Repeating identical imperfections reads as a tile.

Review generated or outsourced work as a contact sheet on the real background. Check light direction, shadow temperature, contour weight, anatomy, horizon and surface scale across the family. Correct the common source of drift before asking for many variations. Archive the chosen key, the rejected alternative and one sentence explaining the choice. Keep editable labels, art exports and source provenance with the kit.

## Carry the scene into UI and movement

A window, ticket or radial selector inherits the world's light and geometry. Give the interactive face its own quiet area even on a rich surface. Decide how that material moves: a brass latch clicks into a stop, paper lifts at a free corner, water carries a reflection a moment longer. Reserve the most elaborate movement for the beat the player should remember. A static mark must carry the meaning when motion is reduced.

For an ending, try an environmental consequence before designing the report: a dark window lights, a ferry pulls away, a signal flag sags or the crowd disperses. Anchor the report to what actually happened, then give it a pace suited to the occasion. If a repeated attempt feels slow, keep the expressive final pose and shorten how the player gets there.
