# Draw and test one control in context

## Borrow a surface from the world

Paint the same control on three materials before picking its finish. On enamel, show a broad bright strip which narrows when the face sinks. On paper, lift a free corner while the pinned corner stays put; allow the ink to darken under the press. On oiled wood, show a rubbed lip where the thumb lands and keep grain out of the arrow's countershape. Retain the same verb, bounds and label through the comparison. A board made from printed card may need only an ink change.

Build an icon family from the game camera: draw the lamp, letter and bell with the same light, line endings and amount of interior detail. Print or view them at actual size, mix two verbs with two inventory objects and remove the labels. Separate objects that disappear into one outline. Restore labels wherever the action remains ambiguous. A hand-drawn surface can carry optically centered, sharply rendered type.

## Decide where the verb lives

For a tile, card or unit, sketch the exact contact point, the visible selection anchor, and the destination. If the action uses a target, expose valid targets while that choice is live. A control that spends currency should show its price beside its verb; a counter that only reports currency should read as a counter. On a short viewport, preserve the decision and its consequence in the same glance. Test the tallest supported numeral and widest translated label.

Start a state strip using one real icon and one long label. Carry the same outer footprint across states. Give pressed depth a stable light source: lower the top face, shorten the shadow and leave the hit rectangle in place. Check the optical center of a lopsided arrow; center the ink mass as well as the bounding box. Freeze halfway through the press to catch clipped labels. The game's material may call for ink filling a stamp or tension in a string instead.

| Moment | Author the response | Probe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Offer / focus | Group verb, cost and affected resource. Distinguish keyboard focus from persistent selection. | Tab in from each side. Hide the pointer. Can the player predict what confirm will do? |
| Press / aim | Acknowledge at the touched object on contact. Declare when a purchase becomes binding. Offset a drag ghost enough to expose the destination under a thumb. | Hold, drift across a neighbor, return, release outside, then try again. Count charges and observe source and destination. |
| Commit / pending | Keep an anchor at the source while authority resolves. Allow unrelated input if safe. Render a resolved purchase from actual state. | Delay a response; double-activate; leave and re-enter. Inspect the ledger as well as the animation. |
| Reject / cancel | Identify the failed constraint: wrong type, insufficient funds, full capacity or expired turn. Indicate the relevant object and the feasible next action. | Compare rejection with successful selection while muted and in grayscale. Verify that cancel restores focus, currency and geometry. |
| Held / depleted | Use shape or a persistent notch alongside tint. Reserve highlight for a useful decision. Reevaluate affordability when the price, space **or** balance changes. | One coin short, exactly affordable, full capacity with a pair, and full capacity without a pair. A suggestion to merge requires a legal pair. |

## Two inputs, one outcome

For a merge, trace pointer-down, drag threshold, target acquisition, release and cancel. Resolve the target under the release location even when the source owns pointer capture. Draw the ghost outside hit testing. A second finger, loss of focus, or leaving the board should leave both units accounted for. For tap-to-select, let tapping the source again back out. With a controller, show a persistent source while focus moves to a candidate. Both paths should exercise the same rules and produce one transaction.

For a destructive choice mapped to the same button as dialogue advance, open the new state on a fresh press. For a fast retry in a precision game, consider the cost of asking for confirmation on every death. Try the actual input cadence before choosing a hold or a modal. See the separate binding decisions in [Celeste](source-notebook.md#celeste).

## Compose a HUD for divided attention

Mark the world anchor for each HUD fact: a hit belongs near its victim, a persistent total belongs in the HUD, and a change traveling between them can connect the two. Keep the final count readable when the flyout is skipped. Test two awards arriving in one frame. Align digits on a stable baseline and hold enough width for the largest expected value. Check negative values, ties and unknown values where the game supports them.

Put a thumb on the action, cover the lower third with a software keyboard, and navigate with a controller unplugged mid-selection if those are supported paths. Open a chat line during the last action. Observe viewport movement, focus and board scale, then inspect the next target. On a shared screen, reject labels that depend on a single player's private knowledge.

## Teach, then withdraw the cue

Pick a safe state with exactly one new relation to learn. In a merge tutorial, provide a real eligible pair and let the player choose either input. Show why the resulting vacancy matters by making the next recruit possible. If they try an invalid match, retain their pieces and highlight the relevant shared trait. Withdraw the prompt after successful performance. On the next board, leave a second pair unmarked and observe whether the player transfers the lesson. Respect an explicit teaching entry point.
