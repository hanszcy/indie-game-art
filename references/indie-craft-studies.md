# Decisions from independent games

Enter through the problem in the current frame. The accounts and sequences below are evidence about their own games. Each experiment is a proposed transfer to a different project. Copy the relationship between an action and an artistic choice. Invent the subject, marks and assets for the new world.

## What makes distance readable?

**Firewatch gives distance its own palette.** In the official audio tour, stop 24, Paolo Surricchio describes an artist-painted strip that assigns fog color by depth. That gives the artists control over the color separating one band of landscape from another. A still can suggest a poster. The production decision is more useful than the poster palette because it holds as the player walks through the landscape. [Campo Santo audio tour, stop 24](https://projectfirewatch.com/cdn/FirewatchAudioTour.pdf)

**Downwell makes color part of a split-second judgment.** Ojiro Fumoto advises players to keep their distance from enemies whose bodies are predominantly red. Amount and placement of the color matter. Spending that same visual area on every rock in a shaft would change what the eye sees while falling. [Fumoto on reading enemies](https://www.pocketgamer.com/downwell/downwell-hints-tips-and-tricks-direct-from-developer-ojiro/)

**Try both on the same hillside.** Paint the far orchard as two merging masses and keep the nearest branch crisp against the sky. Then place a hazard in the busiest part of the canopy. Reserve a distinctive broken contour or a patch of high contrast for its contact edge. Pan the camera and try a dusk palette. If a scenic tree now reads as a hazard, preserve the far color and change its near contour. If everything signals danger equally, the landscape has lost the quiet the player came for.

## What makes a surface feel made and used?

**Lumino City gives light something physical to touch.** State of Play built sets from paper, card and glue, used laser cutting, and worked with miniature lights and motors. Look at the lighting rig and its result together. Layer thickness, the gap at a join and where a lamp fits in a miniature all bound what light can do. A uniform grain layer gives little evidence of scale or construction. [State of Play's illustrated SIGGRAPH account](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/2015-Labs-Fountain_Keeping-It-Real.pdf)

**Venba gives a prop a particular owner and time.** Abhi describes cooking the dishes before making puzzles. The family starts with ingredients more easily available to them in 1980s Toronto and cooks biryani later. The choice of what can sit on this counter already tells part of the story. The fragmented family recipes also give the player something meaningful to reconstruct. [Abhi on building the cooking puzzles](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2023/07/19/venba-interview/)

**Try it on an instrument case.** Give its folded lining a soft occlusion at the seam, the brass catch a narrow moving highlight, and the handle one polished thumb patch. For the owner's history, choose between fresh replacement thread and an old repair worn smooth. Draw both at inventory size. If the repair reads as an unread-message dot, pull it into the stitching and lower its contrast. If the silhouette already carries the history, spend the extra marks on the close-up where someone can enjoy them.

**Test a surface in motion.** During development of *Return of the Obra Dinn*, Lucas Pope compared dither mappings under camera rotation. His sphere-mapped variant reduced visible swimming and introduced edge and sampling compromises. See the sequence from the cube mapping through the side-by-side comparison. For a new woodcut game, hold still, rotate, then translate past a thin mast. If the grain appears to slide over the wood, compare anchoring the marks to the surface with simplifying the midtones. A frozen beauty shot cannot choose between them. [Pope's illustrated fullscreen experiments](https://dukope.com/devlogs/obra-dinn/tig-32/)

## When should a control take over the screen?

**Papers, Please clears a temporary work surface for commitment.** In the mobile devlog's *Stamping* sequence, the passport can be positioned under the stamp heads. Pope specifically wanted pressing down to make the impression. Dropping a dragged stamp would commit on release and lose that physical correspondence. In the published animation, the desk opens, the passport is stamped, then the marked document returns to the ordinary layout. The mark survives the transition. [Pope's mobile prototypes and stamping sequence](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/mobile/)

**Gorogoa keeps related pictures visible for discovery.** Look at PDF pages 29–31 of Jason Roberts' talk. The lower panel with the bowl moves from underneath the bird to underneath the fruit. The upper picture stays readable across the rearrangement. A boundary the player can manipulate becomes part of the puzzle's meaning. On pages 46–47, banners and scaffolding also have jobs in the depicted world. [Roberts' illustrated design talk](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2018/presentations/Roberts_Jason_Gorogoa_design_of.pdf)

**Make the choice on a mending bench.** An awl may fill the view while the player punches a hole. Make its tip meet the material on the committing input and leave the hole visible afterward. Selecting thread to match a faded cuff needs thread and cuff together. An enlarged spool that covers the cuff ruins the comparison. Try both cases on the smallest screen. Restore the normal view, hide instructional copy, and inspect where the evidence of the action lives.

## Which part of an action should move?

**Sable holds a drawing.** Gregorios Kythreotis describes deliberately stepping character animation to give the 3D world a hand-animated quality. Masks, first explored to avoid animating a town full of faces, grew into the world's social roles and became substantial work in their own right. A constraint can give a cast character if it also changes how people inhabit the world. [Kythreotis on animation and masks](https://mcvuk.com/business-news/when-we-made-how-shedworks-went-from-a-north-london-shed-to-the-stunning-dunes-of-sable/)

**Celeste scales the response to the contact.** In the released desktop game's published player source, the ground-collision branch uses downward speed to widen and compress the sprite. A separate threshold gates the dust burst. Recovery approaches the ordinary scale during updates. This lets a light landing and a hard landing differ using the same underlying art. These are code observations, not measured player response times. [Landing branch](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/7fd7ae760bc98bc1659c035d807cb47a3463e9b7/Source/Player/Player.cs#L2554-L2566) · [Scale recovery](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/7fd7ae760bc98bc1659c035d807cb47a3463e9b7/Source/Player/Player.cs#L1165-L1166)

**Ape Out gives the beat an owner.** Gabe Cuzzillo describes cymbals coinciding with kills and Matt Boch's reactive percussion system changing its phrasing with intensity. An instantaneous accent can tell you what just happened while the broader rhythm carries how the room feels. Copying a loud accent onto every footstep would flatten that distinction. [Cuzzillo on the adaptive score](https://nygamecritics.com/2019/04/16/the-circle-interview-creator-gabe-cuzzillo-on-how-ape-out-changed-during-5-years-of-development/)

**Try it on a skier crossing a wooden bridge.** Hold a drawn coat pose for character and keep the skis at their rule-driven position. On landing, bend the knees at contact. Let a loose plank answer a hard landing once. Save the ambient creak for the quiet crossing. Replay a soft landing, a hard landing and a second jump at the earliest allowed instant. With sound muted the landing weight should still differ. With reduced motion the contact pose and resting ski position should still agree. If the bridge keeps exploding under ordinary jumps, reduce the thresholded effect or remove it.

## Take a decision into the next scene

A move often needs several readings. A novice needs to discover what can be manipulated, a practiced player needs a short acknowledgement, and a spectator needs to understand the outcome without seeing the input. Carry forward the specific changed object. A missing tile can show a match to its maker, the tray can record what was served, and the last untouched plate can explain the loss. For a first lesson, stage a safe opportunity to cause the change and let the player make the input. For a frequent retry, retain the evidence and reopen control early. For a rare completion, hold the player's work long enough to look back over it. Compare the two endings from the same starting state.
