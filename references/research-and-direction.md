# Research and direction

## Diagnose before selecting a style

| Observation in the current build | Check | First experiment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The home screen reads like a product landing page | Which object claims attention before play? | Let the central game object anchor the composition. Remove copy that repeats the action. |
| Every region demands equal attention | Relative contrast, borders, scale and motion | Pull secondary panels back while keeping information needed for comparison visible. |
| Controls look borrowed from several kits | Icon weight, camera angle and material in their active states | Draw a single control family in context and compare resting, pressed and exhausted states. |
| Ten characters look interchangeable | Silhouette, posture, body mass and expression | Compare solid silhouettes at play size before adding markings. |
| Detailed art becomes muddy | Frequency of texture and edge contrast at display size | Remove texture away from recognition points. |
| A night scene obscures targets | Values where actors meet the ground | Separate the interactive layer locally and preserve the nighttime mood. |
| A clean scene lacks identity | Recurring shapes or objects tied to play | Pick a distinctive motif that serves a mechanic and repeat it selectively. |
| Everything moves, but play feels inert | Timing and location of action feedback | Give the touched object a readable response and let nearby elements rest. |

Treat these as hypotheses. Management games can need dense panels. An abstract game can use simple geometric marks throughout.

## Study a decision

Choose a question narrow enough to answer, such as how a top-down game distinguishes an untouched tile from a revealed one. Use production screenshots to examine layout, footage to study event order and observed play to judge input. When studying motion, look at the frame before input, the first response, the settled state and the point at which input can resume.

For each reference, keep the source, the kind of evidence, the observed detail, the design inference and the intended edit. Identify anything particular to its camera or rules. A still image cannot establish timing. Preserve gaps honestly when a link, video or build is unavailable.

## Six worked references

**A Short Hike.** Adam Robinson-Yu describes choosing visible pixels, then using flat shading and soft outlines to preserve readability. A production constraint can guide a whole family of decisions. At a low working resolution, put effort into contours and color masses before surface texture. [Developer retrospective](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/08/05/crafting-a-tiny-open-world-a-look-behind-the-scenes-at-the-creation-of-a-short-hike/)

**TUNIC.** Andrew Shouldice describes folding, distressing and scanning a blank physical manual, then compositing its contents for localization. The booklet suits the game's interest in discovery. Carry over the relationship between an interface object and the player's activity, and separate changeable content from material. The fictional script and worn paper belong to TUNIC. [Developer account](https://blog.playstation.com/2022/09/21/the-creation-of-tunics-invaluable-in-game-manual/)

**Unpacking.** Tim Dawson describes an alarm clock that loses its display when picked up and flashes a time when set down. A familiar object's behavior supplies personality and placement feedback at once. Look for a comparable response on an object players actually handle. The account describes the behavior without establishing its timing. [Object design notes](https://blog.playstation.com/2022/05/06/objects-tell-stories-in-unpacking-arriving-on-ps4-and-ps5-may-10/)

**Mini Metro.** The developer describes drawing a transit network using the visual form of an abstract subway map. A diagram can give a game its identity when making and revising that diagram is the player's job. In a management game, preserve information structures needed to make decisions. [Official press kit](https://dinopoloclub.com/press/mini-metro/)

**inbento.** The published screenshot shows food blocks, a central lunchbox, a recipe page and a small tray sharing one visual vocabulary. The box gives the grid a purpose. The recipe page makes a target arrangement intelligible with very little text. Borrow the relationship between objects and functions when it fits the project. [Gameplay screenshot](https://img.itch.zone/aW1nLzY1NTMzNDcucG5n/original/f57qhQ.png) · [Developer page](https://afterburn.itch.io/inbento)

**A Little to the Left.** In the framed-cat screenshot, the picture, frame edges and contact shadow hold the detail against a quiet field. Give the object of attention the richest edges and leave room around it. Other levels and other games can require different densities. [Screenshot](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/635ffc7b70ac56311f9561a6/16874e4a-8892-4f23-9ecc-ea73e5ca1e9d/ALTTL-Catframe.png) · [Studio press kit](https://www.maxinferno.com/press/)

## Compare directions fairly

Keep scene content, camera, target device and scale constant. Identify the decisive variable in each study, such as silhouette, edge material or color organization. Assess recognition, production cost and distinct states. Choose one direction that can survive repeated use. Design original character features, marks and icons. Keep source images as research material unless their terms permit production use.
