# Why the previous skill failed in this application

The prior skill contained useful artistic reasoning, but the implementation treated reading that reasoning as sufficient. It skipped the representative-scene comparison and substituted a shared vector renderer with palette/garnish switches. Automated rules tests passed while the artistic defects remained unexamined.

The 2.0 revision makes the intermediate work inspectable: a direction that predicts visible decisions, an actor/environment/interactable slice, production asset integration, a complete ordinary-action sequence, and a recorded limitation. It permits authored geometry, illustration, pixels and modeled forms; it does not prescribe foxes, cozy palettes or image generation for every game.

Independent review identified two additional ambiguities and they were corrected: semantic native controls do not mean default system styling; repeated inconclusive art revision must expose the unresolved choice rather than claiming perfection or looping indefinitely.

Independent baseline implementations are produced from the functional brief without access to the revised skill or the skill art direction. Shared deterministic mechanics make paired states possible. Effort and assets differ, so this gallery demonstrates examples of a process, not a scientific claim of superiority.

Validation: skill-creator quick_validate passed. A fresh-context task simulation produced an asset-family plan, an actual-scale playable slice, contact-action capture requirements, and an isolated baseline procedure. This proves the instructions are actionable, not that their resulting art is universally preferred.
