# Three playable pairs

The public gallery runs two independent frontends for each game. Each pair shares its rules, data and input contract. The baseline author received a functional brief in a fresh context and built a complete edition without the art skill. The other author used Indie Game Art 2.1 and the same rules. The three pairs were made in sequence. Art direction, time spent and asset production differ. These are examples for people to judge in play, with no player preference or effect size claimed.

## Sokoban

The five maps and push, delivery, undo and completion checks live in the shared engine. The independent baseline draws its own freight depot, worker and crates in Canvas. The other edition puts the player on a night railway platform. A brass-strapped trunk acquires a punched luggage tag when delivered. On a blocked push, the porter moves against the trunk and the trunk stays put.

One original transparent raster atlas was generated with the built-in image generator. The production brief called for a three-quarter railway porter, a squat ochre travel trunk and the same trunk with a baggage tag, in three isolated equal-width cells. Red cap, navy work clothes, brass hardware, warm edge light and economical lithographic hatching connect the subjects. The cutouts were inspected, separated, and exported at smaller sizes after phone captures exposed soft hardware edges. The platform and controls were drawn separately.

The playable checks solved all five maps in both editions using the same sequences of 4, 13, 27, 21 and 22 moves. Screenshots open the second stage. The moving comparison replays the same four moves on the first stage from the same 390 × 740 viewport. The porter's vertical pushes retain the side-view pose.

## Food Match-3

Both editions use six distinct candy shapes, the same 7 × 7 seeded orders, swap validation, cascade events, refills and recovery from a board with no legal moves. The baseline has its own vector candy illustrations and synthesized tones. In the workshop edition, an amber confectioner's mold, handled tools and a paper batch ticket give the board a working context. The order survives the action while each sugar piece retains its identity as it falls.

The built-in image generator supplied six transparent sugar illustrations. The brief specified a three-by-two atlas of red heart drop, orange segment wheel, wrapped yellow bonbon, pressed green leaf, blue star and ridged purple shell, lit from the upper left. Bubbles, sugar-pull marks and irregular silhouettes had to survive at playing size. Another original generated worktop study supplied material for the workshop setting. Clickable cells, the tray details, order totals and animation follow the rules in code. A phone inspection led to a larger goal count and a single gesture cue.

All three orders were completed through the interface in both editions. Identical inputs produced scores of 340, 550 and 470. The moving comparison starts from the opening board and exchanges cells 7 and 8, counted from zero, for six pieces and 60 points. Seeded logic checks included invalid swaps, cascades and naturally occurring shuffles.

## Ski Adventure

The shared 460 m side-scrolling course advances at 60 fixed simulation ticks per second. It includes obstacles, pickups, jumps, optional rotations, upright landing, collision recovery, avalanche pursuit, failure and finishing. Both frontends read the same controller and physics. The baseline draws its own daytime range, huts, rocks, skier and snow. The other edition centers on a bright jacket in a cold polar fjord, with an advancing powder edge, traces at the ski and a distinct compressed landing pose.

Two original raster plates were generated with the built-in image generator. The first requested the same right-facing skier in a saffron anorak and oxblood trousers, drawn in three separate poses on alpha with the ski tips in frame. The second requested a quiet ultramarine fjord panorama, pale mint aurora and angular glaciated peaks, with the live slope, character, obstacles and HUD omitted. The three poses are cropped separately. Their boot positions anchor them to the simulated snow. The near cornice, tracks, bells and avalanche edge are rendered at runtime. At the farthest safe distance, the gap readout remains important for understanding the pursuit.

The static comparison is the same airborne tick 107. The moving comparison replays ticks 52 through 227 using the same short press. A separate ordinary-input run exercised a complete rotation, a bell, a collision and recovery. Both editions passed pause, failure, restart and successful completion. Timed simulations and browser touch emulation supplement visual review. No physical phone session or human preference study was conducted.
