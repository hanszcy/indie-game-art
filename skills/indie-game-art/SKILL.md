# Indie Game Art

Find the instant the player cares about. Someone may be waiting for a voice to answer, reading a rival's intention, or admiring a room they spent an hour arranging. What do they attend to? What should they feel? What must they be able to do next? Work on the actual picture, object, sentence or beat that carries that experience. Preserve the game's rules, player knowledge and approved art unless the brief calls for a change.

## Enter through the work in front of you

| Starting material | First useful move |
| --- | --- |
| Playable rules and stand-in art | Frame a characteristic moment at the shipping camera. Make competing readings of that same moment. Change a major visual decision such as viewpoint within the allowed camera, light distribution, silhouette or mark-making. Compare them where the player spends time. |
| Artwork and an interface that feels like a website | Follow a player making one decision. Locate their evidence, the input and its consequence. Redraw that relationship in context. A permanent table may serve a strategist beautifully. A single object changing under the hand may do more for a tactile game. |
| Good stills and lifeless play | Keep the drawings you like. Find the exact input, decision by the game, visible change and next legal action. Stage the beat at the object that owns it. Try it twice in quick succession. |
| An existing direction that feels ordinary | Point to the part another game could inherit unchanged. Find a specific aspect of this world, character or activity that ought to inhabit that space. Try a new drawing, crop, line of dialogue or held pose there before expanding the revision. |

Match the output to the request. With a screenshot, produce a marked critique or a visual study as appropriate. With source and an implementation request, put the change in its ordinary entry point and exercise it. For a critique-only request, give the artistic diagnosis, the tradeoff and the comparison that would settle it. Work directly on a small edit. A full project benefits from a representative scene before a batch of assets.

## Make an artistic choice someone could disagree with

1. **Find a point of view.** Ask what someone who lives in this world or knows this activity would notice. A real gesture, process, place or tradition can give the game a visual vocabulary of its own. A library during a power cut might feel companionable if pooled light joins two readers, or isolating if a lit desk sits across a gulf of dark carpet. Both can be perfectly legible. Choose according to the relationship the game asks the player to notice.
2. **Spend attention on purpose.** Locate the longest uninterrupted shape, the sharpest edge, the largest interval of quiet or the sound that breaks it. Decide who gets it now and who inherits it on the next beat. Name a nearby detail worth suppressing. In a game about searching, preserve the pleasure of looking and give players a way to resolve uncertainty.
3. **Borrow the reasoning.** When studying another work, identify one observable choice, the conditions that make it work and a change of conditions that would overturn it. A maker's account can support intent. A screenshot can support a judgment about framing. Inspect a sequence or build for timing. Use reference works with different solutions when the decision is open.
4. **Carry the decision through the work.** Put a real actor, ground, interface element and consequence together where the game has them. Check the busiest ordinary state. Make the direction cheap enough to repeat at the requested scale. Keep the quirks that give the piece a voice.
5. **Judge the result in context.** Compare the same moment, device, input and camera. Name the visible gain, the quality you gave up and any unanswered question. Functional checks establish that an edit works. A change of taste needs an actual image, sequence or listening comparison.
