# Build a style someone can draw with

Use this when an identity is new, generic, or rejected on appearance. Keep the process proportional: a small subject study can settle the decision without a full art bible. Preserve an approved style instead of forcing novelty into every task.

## Separate the decisions

| Layer | What it decides | What it cannot establish on its own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject / world | What is depicted and what people do there | A farm, witch's shop or harbor is not yet a style. |
| Visual language | Which proportions, shapes, intervals, contours, omissions and poses construct the picture | A quirky shape does not guarantee appeal or fit. |
| Medium / finish | How ink, paint, pixels, light or modeled surfaces realize those decisions | “Hand-painted gouache”, “cinematic” or “premium” cannot substitute for the drawing. |
| Palette / value | Relationships among visible masses and the player's attention | Saturation, darkness and lighting density are not quality scores. Color-led work still needs specific relationships. |

The layers influence each other; this is a diagnostic, not a rigid production order. A useful direction explains the choices rather than collecting mood adjectives.

## Find a rule that has consequences

Begin with a particular observation, emotional tension or process in the game. Translate it into a few agreements another artist could use to draw something not yet specified. Choose the few that do real work:

- **Proportion and mass:** where weight gathers; which part is disproportionately small, long, blunt, compressed or absent.
- **Contour and negative space:** where curves turn into corners; where an opening separates two masses; which edge disappears.
- **Marks and omissions:** what gets one line rather than a rendered volume; how an ordinary surface is simplified; where detail is intentionally withheld.
- **Composition and scale:** what owns the largest quiet interval; how a small subject stands against its surroundings; how crowding changes the image.
- **Performance:** the characteristic pose, pivot, held interval or deformation that links the object at rest to its action.

“Soft, warm, charming, detailed” cannot decide how to draw the next object. Neither can “add bold outlines” by itself. Make the choices interact. For example, a narrow stem can make a squat mass feel heavy; broad spacing can make a tiny gesture conspicuous. Preserve useful exceptions instead of making every subject the same blob.

Do not demand a world-first invention or copy the signature shapes of a named artist or game. Specific, coherent choices are enough. References supply a decision and its tradeoff, not a borrowed identity.

## Put two credible drawings on the same problem

Keep state, scale and functional affordances matched. Vary construction, spatial relationship or performance, not only grade, props or background. Start with the object or action the player sees repeatedly, rather than a beautiful full-scene backdrop that commits the rest of production too early.

For a crop-sorting puzzle, two possible studies might be folded seed-packet forms whose crease becomes the selection cue, and bent botanical cutouts whose leaf gaps carry identity. Draw the same produce and matched group in both; neither direction is a prescribed farm style. Other assignments may need no crops, cutouts, paper or characters at all.

View a subject beside a confusing neighbor, then repeat it across an ordinary busy board. A strong illustration at 512px may become muddy at 40px; repeated specular accents may become visual noise. Adjust the subject before commissioning a more elaborate frame. A thumbnail, silhouette or simplified-value pass can diagnose where recognition lives. Do not treat grayscale survival as a universal requirement for color-led work.

Select by pointing to visible appeal and fit: a pleasing weight balance, an expressive awkward angle, a clean gap, an interesting rhythm or a satisfying contact. Name what the other drawing does better. Novelty for its own sake and systematic ugliness do not satisfy a request for an appealing game.

## Make the style travel

Draw an ordinary supporting object using the same agreements. If only the hero asset has a voice, the language is incomplete. If every object needs a unique exception, simplify the agreements. If every object becomes interchangeable, restore the differences that matter to play.

Then perform one real action. The treatment should survive selection, contact and the settled state. A bespoke object that receives the same generic pulse and confetti as every other object has an unfinished performance, but stronger effects cannot rescue a rejected drawing.

For generated assets, write the chosen construction decisions and actual footprint into the prompt; “original indie aesthetic, beautiful gouache, rich texture” is not an art brief. Inspect the generated result against the study. Reject a result that replaces the chosen proportions or omissions with a default polished illustration even when it is technically impressive. Tool use, image resolution, asset count and rendering expense are not evidence of a stronger style.

## Respond to “I don't like it” without moving the goalposts

Treat the feedback as evidence about this result. Do not reinterpret it as a universal ban on dark art, illustration, geometry or detail. Identify which visible choice missed: generic construction, conflicting proportions, clutter, muddiness, sterile repetition, weak composition, or an unwanted mood. If the evidence does not distinguish these, offer small visual alternatives rather than asserting a diagnosis as fact.

On the next pass, compare the affected subject and its neighbors before surrounding scenery. Show what changed in the drawing. Separately report whether it works, what artistic judgment you made, and what remains untested. Do not declare the aesthetic problem solved merely because the new work complies with this document. The user's preference is not overruled by an internal rubric.
