# Study and judge a direction

## Turn a source into a usable choice

Start from the question the team must settle. Search for a passage, production account, playable moment or illustrated development decision that could steer it. Award archives and well-liked games can widen the search. A review score identifies affection for a whole game. It cannot say whether a particular gradient, framing choice or camera shake caused that affection.

Separate what the artifact shows, what its maker reports and what you infer. Keep a locator such as the named object, frame, timestamp or paragraph with the claim. Compare a second work when the first encourages a style habit. Ask under what condition the opposite decision would be better. Translate the answer into a change you can make to an original piece.

**An example of reasoning across sources.** The layered atmosphere in *Firewatch* lets distance change the color of the scene. The hand-placed light in *INSIDE* gives an artist fine control of a particular surface. Both could help with a distant figure. If the point of play is judging a journey, try separating planes with atmosphere. If the point is noticing a person hesitate at a doorway, try letting one hand catch the light. An equal wash over the whole corridor would spend contrast on the wrong subject. Trace the distant and local edges while the camera moves to see which claim survives. The proposed treatments are judgments for the new scene. [Firewatch Audio Tour, stop 30, pp. 65–66](https://projectfirewatch.com/cdn/FirewatchAudioTour.pdf#page=65) · [Playdead's lighting notes](https://blog.playdead.com/articles/the_lighting_of_inside/lighting_of_inside.html)

Theory helps when it changes the drawing. The MDA framework links a rule to the behavior it encourages and the experience this produces. Work backward from a desired feeling to what the player would actually attend to and do. Doug Church's account of intention and perceivable consequence helps locate the missing link when an interface disappoints. Show a still just before commitment if the player cannot form a plan. Replay the result if they can plan but cannot tell what they accomplished. In exploratory or unsettling games, name the uncertainty worth preserving too. [Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek on MDA](https://www.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/pubs/MDA.pdf) · [Doug Church's design tools](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/formal-abstract-design-tools)

## Compare the artwork, not the presentation budget

Keep camera, actual display size, rule state and input matched between studies. Where possible use the same source drawing or material budget in both. Two plausible directions reveal more than a deliberately weak baseline. If the choice is uncertain, change the consequential variable first. Establishing the identity of an entire game may need a few related decisions to move together.

| Suspected weakness | Comparison that can change the edit |
| --- | --- |
| Generic visual identity | Cover the title and promotional copy. Compare the same location or abstract pattern at rest. Point to what could only belong to this game's world, rule or narrator. Keep deliberate anonymity if the scene needs it. |
| Attractive individual assets, weak composed frame | Squint or reduce the picture. Where do the two strongest shapes meet? Then restore normal size. Is there a rewarding secondary discovery where the eye naturally travels? |
| Clear frame, wrong mood | Keep the cues equally readable. Try a larger empty interval, two figures sharing a shadow, a change of mark or a new relationship between foreground and horizon. Ask what each image makes the viewer expect to happen. |
| Interface drawing attention away from play | Identify the evidence a player must retain. Cover the panel or hide the candidate line. Replay the choice. Restore what affects agency or voice, then move it closer to the moment it matters. |
| Polished movie, awkward interaction | Observe input, confirmed result and next legal input in ordinary use. Interrupt mid-effect, repeat it and inspect the settled state. Compare reduced motion and muted play where those channels carry information. |
| Direction expensive or fragile across content | Make one difficult neighbor, crop, language or lighting condition. A beautiful treatment that only works on the hero asset needs a less costly companion treatment. |

When someone is available to play, ask a neutral question such as “What changed?” or “What do you expect if you do that again?” A usability answer and a taste judgment can diverge. Someone can understand the scene perfectly and still find its performance shrill. In a solo review, identify your own preference and point to the frame, edge or beat that prompted it. Keep untested audience claims open.

Deliver the work in the requested form. For a still, show the image at its intended scale. For motion, show the before, event and settled state in sequence. For a build, exercise the normal player entry point with the same action on both versions. State the real constraint and the tradeoff in ordinary language. Only carry a reusable art agreement into project documentation when another scene or contributor will need it.
