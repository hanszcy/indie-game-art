# Edit the place where the player acts

Open the game at its ordinary camera. Cover the title, surrounding panels and far scenery with a piece of paper. Look at what remains under the player's finger or gaze. Does its shape say anything about what it is made of, who uses it or what it is about to do? Capture the defect before deciding what to draw. A word game might reveal a compelling arrangement of letters. Another game might reveal identical floating tokens on a featureless field.

## Find a visual idea that can generate work

Choose a process worth observing closely. Look for a distinguishing physical or graphic fact that survives at the smallest play size. A printing impression has a relief face and a registered mark left on the sheet. A line of dancers can hold one repeated pose until a single body falls out of step. Derive the interactive surface, the acting subject and one consequence from the same fact. Leave unrelated scenery quiet. Refer to the project's own subject before reaching for familiar decorative motifs.

Draw two plausible treatments with equal rules and camera. Make the difference visible inside the action area. One could depend on mass and contact, another on the gaps between objects. Put the important pieces together before rendering details. Evaluate one drawing with the HUD covered and another with the player's hand over the control. The choice needs an artistic reason as well as a readable next move.

## Stage one ordinary action, then its opposite

Look at the object both before contact and after every effect has gone. What lasted? Where does the eye go next? Draw that settled relationship first, then decide how the transition earns it. A broad shape does the work of identifying an object. A small, local change can show who acted on it. A failed attempt should retain the previous truth and can expose a different quality of the same material. Choose a meaningful variation such as compression, occlusion, broken spacing or release of tension. Let framing and effects follow the rule's event.

Use an ordinary, unposed capture to choose the next revision. If the environment carries more drawing than the piece the player handles, spend the next pass on that piece and its contact with the world. If every state has the same gleam or bounce, replace one universal effect with a change specific to the material. When motion is reduced, leave a legible shape, spatial relation or typographic change at rest.

## Give attention back to play

Read the visible copy while performing the task. Keep words the player needs for action, access, story or a consequential decision. Remove the caption that announces an animation the player just saw. Condense secondary controls until the primary field gets space to breathe. Leave orientation, keyboard focus and touch targets usable.

For a comparison, replay the same legal inputs at the same scale on both editions, including an awkward moment. Capture ordinary play and the settled result. Let someone launch either game from the pictures. A more elaborate picture can still be the less readable one. State the observed difference and the untested taste judgment separately.
