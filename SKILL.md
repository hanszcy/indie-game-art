# Indie Game Art

Start with a moment you can point to. What is the player trying to do, what do they need to see or hear, and what should it feel like? Preserve the rules, camera and approved assets unless the brief changes them. Use the answer to direct one consequential edit, then judge it in the game.

## Choose a starting point

| What exists today | First piece of work | What would change the decision? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Playable rules, placeholder art** | Take the same playable frame through two treatments. For a climbing game, one could reveal the route through overlapping ridges, another through the wear left by previous climbers. Draw a real foothold and a decorative crack in both. Carry the stronger choice into the busiest view. [Depth, color and materials](references/indie-craft-studies.md#what-makes-distance-readable) | If the landscape feels vast but players step on the decorative crack, keep the distant treatment and redraw the local edge. |
| **Finished screens that feel like a website** | Trace one transaction. Find where the player's hand or focus goes, which two things must be compared, and where the consequence belongs. Prototype that interaction inside the scene. [Two very different uses of a frame](references/indie-craft-studies.md#when-should-a-control-take-over-the-screen) | If the beautiful object conceals a value needed for the decision, keep the values together and give the object a smaller role. |
| **Finished art, lifeless play** | Take one frequent action. Mark input, rule commit, first visible response and the next legal input on the same timeline. Animate the part that bears the force. Keep the final pose legible with sound and motion removed. [Character timing and event accents](references/indie-craft-studies.md#which-part-of-an-action-should-move) | If a second input is legal while the first effect is playing, try it. If players read the flourish as another success, shorten its tail or move the accent. |

For a mixed brief, follow the break that affects a real decision first. For a still-only brief, return the visual comparison and identify the one behavior that needs a playable check. If the rules exist but the build is unavailable, a small interaction study can replay named outcomes. Keep the input request separate from the game-confirmed result and disclose which events the study supplies. [A tested example with cloth and a wooden peg](references/cloth-field-study.md)

## Use theory to make a choice

Choose the question that could change the art. MDA helps connect a desired feeling to something players actually do. Intention and perceivable consequence separate a confusing invitation from a confusing result. Perceptual grouping helps decide which marks belong together. Game Feel helps place feedback on the correct event. Each lens has a worked example and a way to disprove the proposed treatment. [Four lenses for art direction](references/design-lenses.md)

Study a specific moment in a creator's account, frame sequence or build. Distinguish what the source shows from what you infer. When two works give opposite answers to a brief, say which condition in this game makes one answer fit. Deliver the changed frame or action where the available materials allow it. Use the references to guide execution and keep the research recap proportionate to the request.

For a project that needs a broader visual vocabulary, the [TGA and Steam study guide](references/award-and-player-studies.md) indexes works by concrete decisions, from a single color added to a cloak to revealing an opponent's next move. Follow a creator source for the moment closest to the brief. Look beyond the selection when its cameras, production budgets or player activities do not fit.

## Pick the scale

| Assignment | Useful deliverable |
| --- | --- |
| One rough control, sprite or effect | Edit that object against its actual neighbors. Compare at play size, at rest and in use. |
| Find a look | Paint comparable keys of the same subject and camera. Identify what each direction buys, costs and risks losing. Label concepts as studies. |
| Establish a world | Choose a visual premise, representative gameplay frame and small family of materials and silhouettes. Carry the approved language into the requested scenes. |
| Polish a playable game | Find the frame where its character or mood breaks. Make a reversible art edit and replay its entrance, busiest moment and exit. |

## Develop the look

1. **Name the emotional change.** What does this place feel like on arrival, at peak attention and after the action? Keep the player's verb, camera, display size and approved art in view. Use the verb to decide which shapes deserve attention. For an art brief, keep existing rules and balance as constraints.
2. **Study a decision.** Pick references for specific questions such as how a low ceiling presses into a silhouette or how a paper menu catches the light. Record the actual source and which features belong to that work. [Art-direction studies](references/research-and-direction.md) · [Materials and light in Dead Cells](references/source-notebook.md#dead-cells-environments) · [Character relationships in Neva](references/source-notebook.md#neva)
3. **Make a visual argument.** Compare massing, depth, key light, edge treatment and material at thumbnail size. Describe what the eye meets first and what it discovers on a second look. Try removing one motif or effect before adding another. [Look-development workshop](references/look-development.md) · [Original color script and drawings](assets/studies/README.md)
4. **Art-direct in context.** Put the real character, background, type and control in one playable frame. For scenery, compare a quiet frame with the most crowded one. For controls, exercise pressed, busy and exhausted states. Adjust shapes, light and surface at the target size before multiplying assets. [Visual production](references/visual-production.md) · [Visual contract template](assets/visual-contract.md)
5. **Carry the feeling through time.** Let a selected object, character pose, effect or change in ambience express the event. Resolve back to a frame the player can read. Let a victory breathe where a repeated miss deserves a short recovery. Check reduced motion, muted sound and rapid input where relevant.

## Go deeper where the picture breaks

| Where the brief leads | Working reference |
| --- | --- |
| Busy backgrounds, anonymous props, muddy palette, generic atmosphere | [Light, edge, palette, place and motif](references/look-development.md) |
| The art misses the game's central activity, scale or camera | [Frame the verb](references/play-direction.md) |
| Controls feel like a software toolbar | [Control materials and state strips](references/control-craft.md) and [UI and motion](references/ui-and-motion.md) |
| Actors float, hits lack weight or every effect competes | [Pose, FX, camera and mix](references/motion-and-mix.md) |
| Home, lobby, discovery, win and loss all look like the same panel | [Scene handoffs](references/scene-handoffs.md) |
| A choice must survive more assets, a new locale or a production handoff | [Asset production](references/visual-production.md), [review in play](references/review-and-iteration.md) and [study sheet](assets/study-sheet.md) |

Use only the references needed for the edit. Deliver the requested stage: a look study, production assets or an implemented change. Label authored examples, observed behavior and proposed experiments accurately. A technical pass can establish that the art renders and reacts correctly. Approval of the look comes from judging the actual images and playable moments. [Project lessons](references/session-lessons.md)
